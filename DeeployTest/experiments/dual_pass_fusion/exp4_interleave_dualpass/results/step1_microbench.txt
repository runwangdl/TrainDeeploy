CYCLE DECOMPOSITION (len=256, both acc settings; the two rows give the same split)
  acc=1: SEQ-ILV = 395,260 cycles = 262,144 eliminated load instrs + 131,072 eliminated load stalls
  acc=8: SEQ-ILV = 393,176 cycles = 262,144 eliminated load instrs + 131,072 eliminated load stalls
  262,144 = 2 x 256 x 512 = exactly one activation load per stream-iteration.
  => ~2/3 of the win is REDUNDANT LOAD ELIMINATION, ~1/3 is LOAD-USE STALL FILLING.

CONTROL THAT FAILED (recorded because it changes the interpretation)
  A third variant ILV_NS was written to load act[i] separately in each stream, to isolate pure
  scheduling from operand sharing. It measured IDENTICAL to ILV_SH at every point (e.g. len=256
  acc=1: 666,209 vs 666,205 cycles, same instr). clang common-subexpression-eliminates the second
  load once the two streams share a loop body. The two mechanisms therefore cannot be separated at
  source level -- interleaving *causes* the load sharing. Report them as one combined effect.

IMISS is flat (~90-200) across every variant: at these sizes the loop body stays resident in the
512 B L0, so this microbenchmark does NOT exercise the I-cache pressure that dominates exp3's real
conv (22.8 % of its cycles). That risk is untested here and must be measured on the real kernel.
