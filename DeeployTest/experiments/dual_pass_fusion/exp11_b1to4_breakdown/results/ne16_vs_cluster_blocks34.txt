Why: the NE16 prep cost scales with the WEIGHT (Co x K), the NE16 gain with OUTPUT PIXELS x weight. Blocks 3-4 have the
largest weights (3,584 / 7,168 per pass) and the fewest pixels (40 / 10 per pass): the accelerator's prep is paid in full
and amortised over almost no work. On block 4 the NE16 node is not even faster than the cluster's conv: NE16 itself
computes the 4 jobs in 4,506 cycles, but the per-job fixed costs (column-build fork ~1.3 k, task set-up + dispatch
~1.5 k per job) are 11.5 k; one of the two jobs per pass computes a single pixel (1x1 remainder job, 1 of 9 subtile
pixels used).

Kx1 handling (blocks 3-4, kernel 7x1): K = 7 x Ci, one tap = Ci contiguous bytes in HWC, the column is (ky, kx, ci) =
(ky, ci); NE16DualPerturbEncode runs with hwc=1, kh=7, kw=1, i.e. the same order; the bias row sum is order-free. pads are
0 for these blocks. Exact against the oracle in every run (0/2560, 0/640: plain, microbench, probe pipelined and
sequential). The prep perturbation matches the cluster's to 2 % (same routine, same work), so no path does extra or
wrong work for Kx1.
